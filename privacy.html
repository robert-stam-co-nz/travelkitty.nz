Privacy Policy

Travel Kitty · last updated 9 September 2026

Travel Kitty is a shared travel-expense tracker. You record what a trip costs, optionally with other people, and the app works out who owes what. This policy describes exactly what the app stores, where it goes, and how to get rid of it.

Who is responsible

Travel Kitty is made by an individual developer, not a company. Questions about this policy or about your data go to support@travelkitty.nz.

What the app collects

Only what you type into it, plus what is needed to sign you in — and, if you subscribe, what Google Play tells us about that subscription, which is described under Subscriptions and payment below.

The app does not collect your contacts, your device's location in the background, your browsing, or advertising identifiers. There is no analytics or advertising SDK in it, and nothing is sold or shared for marketing.

Location, specifically

The app asks for location permission only when you choose to pin an expense to a place, and it uses your position at that moment to centre the map. It does not track you, does not read your location in the background, and works normally if you refuse the permission — you can drag the map to the spot instead.

Who else can see it

People on the same trip. That is the point of the app: if you add someone to a trip, they can see that trip's expenses, amounts, descriptions, categories, photos, pinned locations, and your display name and avatar colour. They cannot see your email address, your other trips, or anything else about your account.

Where it is stored

On your device, and on Google Firebase in the us-east1 region (South Carolina, United States). Travel Kitty is used by travellers everywhere; a single region has to be chosen when the database is created and cannot be changed afterwards, and this one is the closest compromise for people across the Americas and Europe. This means your information is stored outside New Zealand. Specifically:

  • Firebase Authentication — your email address and sign-in credentials. Passwords are held by Google, hashed; the developer never sees them.
  • Cloud Firestore — trips, expenses, categories, balances.
  • Cloud Storage — photos you attach.
  • Firebase App Check — verifies requests come from the real app rather than a script.

Google processes this data as a service provider. Their handling is covered by the Firebase privacy documentation. Data is encrypted in transit.

Google Maps is separate from the above: it draws the maps and is not where anything is stored. See "Other services the app talks to" below.

Other services the app talks to

  • Google Maps — the maps are drawn by the Google Maps SDK. When you open one, it contacts Google to fetch map data, which necessarily reveals your device's IP address and which part of the world you are looking at, and Google receives information about that use under its own privacy policy. No account data, no expense amounts and no trip details are sent. Google's own "show my location" layer is switched off in this app, so the SDK is not given your position — the "pin where I am" button asks the operating system instead, and that coordinate stays on your device unless you save the pin.
  • Exchange rates — when an expense is in a currency other than the trip's, the app fetches a rate from a public exchange-rate API. The request contains only the currency codes: no personal data, no amounts.
  • Google Cloud (Gemini) — only when you import a bank statement on Premium, and only to work out what kind of business each shop name is. What is sent is a list of shop names; see Sorting an imported statement below, which sets out what is sent and what is not.
  • Firebase Crashlytics — when the app crashes or hits an error it could not handle, a report is sent so the fault can be found and fixed. It contains the technical detail of the failure: the stack trace, the type of device, the version of Android and of the app, and how long the app had been running. It also carries an installation identifier, which is generated by the app and is not your account, your email address or anything you typed. Your trips, expenses, amounts, photos and pinned locations are not in a crash report. Reports are sent automatically and there is currently no way to turn them off from inside the app; if you would rather not send them, that is a reason not to install it, and one worth telling us about at the address above.

Sorting an imported statement

Premium lets you import a statement from your bank. When you do, the app can work out what kind of business each shop is, so the expenses arrive already sorted into categories rather than all in one pile.

To do that it sends a list of the shop names in your file, and the currency each was billed in, to Google's Gemini model running on Google Cloud — the same Google Cloud that already stores your trips. Where your bank puts its own category label on a row, that label is sent too, as a starting point the model can correct.

The amounts, the dates, your balance and your account number are not sent, and neither is anything about who paid or which trip it is for. What leaves your phone is a list of shop names. That is a deliberate limit rather than an incidental one: a list of names is a much smaller thing to disclose than a financial history, and the app is built so that sending more is not an option it has.

Google processes that list on our instructions, as a service provider, and does not use it to train their models — the same relationship described under "Where it is stored" above, on the same Google Cloud terms. Nothing is sent for a statement you do not import, and nothing is sent on the free plan.

A shop the model cannot place is left uncategorised rather than guessed at, and you can categorise it yourself. Correcting a category teaches the app, not the model. The correction is saved as a rule on your own account and applied before anything is sent next time, so a shop you have already named is never asked about again.

Subscriptions and payment

We never see how you pay. Premium is bought through Google Play, and Google takes the payment. Your card number, your billing address and everything else you give Google stay with Google: none of it passes through this app and none of it is stored on our servers. Google is the seller, and the subscription is an agreement between you and them.

What we do store, against your account, is what Google tells us about the subscription itself:

  • a purchase token — an opaque string Google issues to identify the purchase;
  • which plan was bought, monthly or yearly;
  • whether it is currently active, and when the paid period ends.

That is what lets the app know an account is entitled to Premium. It is checked with Google on our server rather than worked out on your phone, because an app that could grant itself a paid plan is an app anybody could grant themselves a paid plan with.

We also keep a record linking that purchase token to your account, for a dull but necessary reason: when Google tells us a subscription has renewed, lapsed, been cancelled or been refunded, the message names the token and not the person. Without the link there would be no way to know whose subscription had changed. Those messages from Google contain no payment details either.

Deleting your account does not cancel your subscription. It keeps renewing, and Google keeps charging, until you cancel it yourself in Google Play: Play Store → your profile → Payments and subscriptions. This app cannot cancel it for you, and does not do it quietly on your behalf — the agreement is between you and Google. If you are leaving, cancel there as well as deleting here, or you will go on paying for an app you no longer have.

Deleting your account does remove the records described above.

How long it is kept

Until you delete it. Deleting an expense removes it for everyone on the trip. Deleting a trip you own removes it, its expenses and its photos for everyone on it. Deleting your account is described below.

Beyond that, we keep your information for as long as your account is active. We may remove data belonging to accounts that have been inactive for an extended period, rather than hold somebody's records indefinitely after they have stopped using Travel Kitty. We will write to the email address on the account before removing anything.

A photo you take in the app is also saved to your own phone. It goes into your photo library, in an album called Travel Kitty, at the moment you take it — so it is backed up by whatever already backs up your photos, and it stays yours whatever happens to your account or to this app. That copy is not ours and we cannot reach it. Photos you pick from your library are already there, and are not duplicated.

Photos are the exception, and only on a paid plan. Storing photos on our servers is part of what a subscription pays for. If a subscription ends, we may remove uploaded photos from our servers after a period, rather than store them indefinitely for an account that is no longer subscribed. We do not set that period here, because it depends on how much is being stored and for how many people, but two things hold whatever we choose: we will write to the email address on the account before removing anything, and there will be a reasonable opportunity to save copies first — though for photos taken in the app there is already a copy in your own library, put there when you took it.

That applies to photos only. Ending a subscription does not by itself remove your trips, expenses, amounts, categories or settlements: those follow the same rule as everything else above, and stay while the account is in use. Going back to the free plan is not a reason to delete anybody's record of a trip.

Deleting your account and data

In the app: Settings → Profile → Delete account. This permanently removes your profile, your custom categories, your sign-in credentials, and every trip you own — including that trip's expenses and photos, for all of its members. Trips owned by someone else are left alone; you are removed from them, and expenses you added to them remain, because the trip's balances would not add up without them.

It also removes the subscription records described above — the purchase token, the plan, its state and expiry, and the link between the token and your account.

It does not cancel a Premium subscription, and nothing here can. That is an agreement between you and Google: it renews, and charges, until you cancel it in Google Play — Play Store → your profile → Payments and subscriptions. Do that as well, whichever order you like, or you will keep paying for an app you have deleted. See Subscriptions and payment.

If you cannot get into the app, see the account deletion page, which explains how to request deletion by email.

Your rights

You can see everything the app holds about you from inside it, export a trip's expenses to a CSV file (Settings → Import & export data), correct anything by editing it, and delete any of it. If you would rather ask for a copy or a correction by email, write to support@travelkitty.nz.

Children

Travel Kitty is not directed at children and is not designed for them.
