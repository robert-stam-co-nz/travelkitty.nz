Privacy policy

Travel Kitty · last updated 23 November 2026

Travel Kitty is a shared travel expense app. You keep track of what a trip costs, on your own or with other people, and Travel Kitty works out who owes what. This page sets out exactly what we store, where it goes, and how to get rid of it.

Who we are

Travel Kitty is made by one individual developer, not a company. If you have a question about this policy or about your data, email support@travelkitty.nz.

What we collect

Only what you type in, plus what we need to sign you in, and a few things set out below.

Each of those has its own section. If you subscribe, we keep what Google Play tells us about your subscription; see Subscriptions and payment. If you set up your own bank connection through Akahu, we keep what you import; see Connecting your own bank through Akahu. If you scan a receipt, we send the photo of it to be read; see Scanning a receipt.

If you say yes to sharing how you use the app, Travel Kitty also sends some counts to Google Analytics; see Counting how the app is used.

Your location

Travel Kitty asks for permission to use your location only when you choose to pin an expense to a place. It uses where you are at that moment to centre the map. It doesn’t track you, and it doesn’t read your location in the background. If you say no, it works normally: you can drag the map to the spot instead.

On Premium, you can also find a place by typing its name. When you do, Travel Kitty sends what you typed to Google, along with where you are at that moment, so Google can suggest places near you. If it doesn’t know where you are, it sends the point the map is showing instead. See Google Places below. Nothing is sent if you just tap the map, and nothing is sent on the Free plan.

Who else can see it

The people on the same trip. That’s the point of Travel Kitty. Once someone joins your trip, they can see that trip’s expenses, amounts, descriptions, categories, pinned locations and cover photo, plus your display name and avatar colour. On Premium, they also see your place photos. On Free, those stay on your phone.

They can’t see your email address, your other trips, or anything else about your account. They can’t see a receipt you scan either: it’s private to you, even though the expense it belongs to is shared.

The one exception is a request to join a trip. Until the trip’s owner answers it, the owner sees your email address, so they can check it’s you.

Where it’s stored

On your phone, and on Google Firebase in the us-east1 region (South Carolina, United States). This means your information is stored outside New Zealand.

Why there? People use Travel Kitty all over the world. A database has to be given one region when it’s created, and that can’t be changed afterwards. We chose this one as the closest compromise for people across the Americas and Europe.

Google Analytics is the exception, if you say yes. Google keeps those counts in its own data centres, and we can’t choose where, so they may be outside the United States.

If you sign in at view.travelkitty.nz, your browser also keeps a copy of the trips you’re on, and the expenses of any you open. Signing out doesn’t clear that copy, so on a computer you share, use a private window.

In detail:

  • Firebase Authentication: your email address and sign-in details. Google holds passwords in hashed (scrambled) form, and we never see them.
  • Cloud Firestore: trips, expenses, categories, balances.
  • Cloud Storage: trip cover photos, and on Premium, place photos and backed-up receipts.
  • Firebase App Check: checks that requests come from the real app rather than a script.

Google handles this data for us as a service provider. How it does that is covered by the Firebase privacy documentation. Data is encrypted while it travels.

Google Maps is separate from all of this: it draws the maps, and nothing is stored there. See “Other services Travel Kitty uses” below.

Other services Travel Kitty uses

  • Google Maps: Google Maps draws the maps. When you open one, it contacts Google to fetch the map. That means Google sees your device’s IP address and which part of the world you’re looking at, and it receives information about that use under its own privacy policy. No account details, expense amounts or trip details are sent. Google’s own “show my location” layer is turned off in Travel Kitty, so Google Maps isn’t given where you are. The Use my current location button asks your phone instead. That position stays on your phone unless you save the pin or search for a place by name (next item).
  • Google Places: only on Premium, and only when you type in Place name while pinning an expense. What you type is sent to Google with where you are, or the point the map is showing, so Google can suggest places nearby. When you pick one, Travel Kitty asks Google exactly where it is. Google receives this under its own privacy policy. No account details, expense amounts or trip details are sent.
  • Exchange rates: when an expense is in a different currency from the trip’s, Travel Kitty gets a rate from a public exchange-rate service. The request holds only the currency codes: no personal details and no amounts.
  • Akahu: only if you set it up yourself. Akahu is a New Zealand service that links apps to banks. See Connecting your own bank through Akahu below.
  • Google Cloud (Gemini): for two things only. When you scan a receipt, on either plan, the photo of it is sent to be read; see Scanning a receipt below. When you import transactions through Akahu on Premium, a list of shop names is sent so we can work out what kind of business each one is; see Sorting imported transactions below. Each section sets out what is sent and what isn’t.
  • Firebase Crashlytics: when Travel Kitty crashes or hits an error it couldn’t handle, it sends a report so we can find and fix the problem. The report holds the technical details of what went wrong: where in the code it failed (the stack trace), the type of device, the version of Android and of Travel Kitty, and how long it had been running. It also carries an installation ID, which the app makes itself. It isn’t your account, your email address or anything you typed. Your trips, expenses, amounts, photos and pinned locations aren’t in a crash report. Reports are sent automatically, and for now there’s no way to turn them off inside the app. If you’d rather not send them, that’s a reason not to install it, and one we’d like to hear about at the address above.
  • Google Analytics for Firebase: off unless you say yes. If you do, Travel Kitty sends Google counts of what’s done in the app, never your name, email address or what’s in your trips; see Counting how the app is used.

Counting how the app is used (Google Analytics)

This is off unless you say yes. Travel Kitty asks once, on My trips, and asks again if you reinstall it or move to a new phone. You can change your answer any time in Settings → Share how you use Travel Kitty.

If you say yes, Travel Kitty tells Google when you do certain things in the app: start a trip, add an expense, share or open an invite link, ask to join a trip or answer a request, import from your bank, or sign in. Google also records when the app is open and for how long, the versions of Travel Kitty and Android, your phone’s model, and a rough location it works out from your IP address, such as your city or region, not your exact position. If you buy Premium, it records the plan and its price. Google already knows those from Play, and no card or billing details are involved. Each installation of Travel Kitty gets an identifier from Google for this, separate from the crash-report one.

We never send your name, your email address or what’s in your trips: no amounts, descriptions, places, photos or who’s on them. There’s no advertising identifier and no advertising.

Google keeps the detailed records for two months. Our reports show only totals, such as how many trips were started in a week, and can go back further. The records aren’t linked to your account, so deleting your account doesn’t delete them; they expire on their own. Google handles this for us as a service provider, and sharing with Google’s other products is turned off. See How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Connecting your own bank through Akahu

Travel Kitty has no bank connection of its own. If you bank in New Zealand and want your transactions brought in, you can set one up yourself, on either plan, through Akahu, a New Zealand service that links apps to banks. You make your own personal app with Akahu, and it gives you two tokens. You enter those in Settings → Advanced → Akahu. If you don’t do that, nothing in this section applies to you.

Your arrangement with Akahu is your own. You connect your bank on Akahu’s site, under Akahu’s own terms. Travel Kitty isn’t part of that step, and neither the app nor our server ever sees your bank username or password.

Your tokens stay on your phone. They’re kept in your phone’s secure storage, under your Travel Kitty account, and they’re only ever sent to Akahu. They aren’t on our servers or in your Travel Kitty account, so we can’t read them. Someone else signing in on the same phone won’t see them, and on another phone you’ll need to enter them again. Anyone who has them can read your bank transactions, so don’t share them.

Your transactions don’t pass through our servers on the way in. When you import, your phone asks Akahu directly for the settled transactions on the accounts you ticked, for the dates shown. Your balances and account numbers aren’t saved.

Importing adds those transactions to the trip you imported them into, as expenses or as money in. Each one holds the date, the amount, the currency and the bank’s description, along with Akahu’s own reference for the transaction and the account. Everyone on that trip can see it, like any other expense.

Where the bank gives the account number of whoever paid you, we keep a fingerprint of that number, never the number itself, so Travel Kitty can recognise the same payer next time. If you delete an imported expense, the trip keeps Akahu’s reference for it, so the next import doesn’t bring it back. Your phone also remembers how far each account has been imported.

To stop, go to Settings → Advanced → Akahu and choose Remove from this phone. Expenses you’ve already imported stay on their trips. That only removes the tokens from Travel Kitty. To end Akahu’s access to your bank, do that in your Akahu account.

Sorting imported transactions

When you import transactions through Akahu on Premium, Travel Kitty can work out what kind of business each shop is, so the expenses arrive already sorted into categories rather than all in one pile.

To do that, it sends a list of the shop names, and the currency each was charged in, to Google’s Gemini model on Google Cloud. That’s under the same Google Cloud terms that cover your trips. For money coming in, it sends the payer’s name instead of a shop’s.

This one request isn’t limited to the us-east1 region where the rest of your data is stored. Google runs its current models from one global service and sends each request to whichever data centre can answer it, so the shop names may be processed outside the United States. We don’t keep the list, and your trips, expenses and photos stay in us-east1. Google may log what it’s sent to check for abuse of its service, under its Google Cloud terms.

The amounts, the dates, your balance and your account number aren’t sent, and neither is anything about who paid or which trip it’s for. What leaves your phone is a list of shop names. We built it that way on purpose. A list of names gives away far less than a financial history, and Travel Kitty is built so it can’t send more.

Google processes that list on our instructions, as a service provider, and doesn’t use it to train its models. That’s the same arrangement described under “Where it’s stored” above, on the same Google Cloud terms. Nothing is sent for transactions you don’t import, and sorting sends nothing on the Free plan.

If the model can’t place a shop, it’s left in Uncategorised rather than guessed at, and you can pick a category yourself. Correcting a category teaches Travel Kitty, not the model. Your correction is saved as a rule on your own account and applied before anything is sent next time, so the model is never asked again about a shop you’ve already named.

Scanning a receipt

When you tap Scan a receipt on Add expense, Travel Kitty reads the photo and fills in the amount, currency, date, shop and category for you to check. Nothing is saved until you save the expense.

To read it, we send the photo of the receipt through our server to Google’s Gemini model on Google Cloud, along with the trip’s currency and dates and the names of its categories. This happens on both plans, and only when you scan.

A receipt can show more than the total. Whatever is printed on it goes with the photo, such as the last digits of your card, your name or a loyalty number. If you’d rather not send that, type the expense in instead.

Your name, your email address, the trip’s name and your other expenses aren’t sent. Our server keeps no copy of the photo or of what the model read from it, and neither goes in our logs.

Like bank sorting, this request isn’t limited to us-east1, so the photo may be processed outside the United States. Google processes it on our instructions, as a service provider, and doesn’t use it to train its models. Google may log what it’s sent to check for abuse of its service, under its Google Cloud terms.

Where the receipt is kept. On the Free plan, it stays on your phone. On Premium, it’s also backed up to a private folder that only your account can read. Either way, the other people on the trip can’t see it, and it isn’t added to your photo library. Deleting the expense removes its receipt, whoever deletes the expense. So does removing the receipt from the expense.

Subscriptions and payment

We never see how you pay. You buy Premium through Google Play, and Google takes the payment. Your card number, your billing address and everything else you give Google stay with Google. None of it passes through Travel Kitty, and none of it is stored on our servers. Google is the seller, and the subscription is an agreement between you and Google.

What we do keep, against your account, is what Google tells us about the subscription itself:

  • a purchase token: a code Google gives each purchase to identify it;
  • which plan was bought, monthly or yearly;
  • whether it’s active now, and when the paid period ends.

That’s how Travel Kitty knows an account has Premium. Our server checks it with Google, rather than your phone working it out, because if the app could give itself Premium, anybody could.

We also keep a record linking that purchase token to your account. The reason is dull but necessary. When Google tells us a subscription has renewed, lapsed, been cancelled or been refunded, the message names the token, not the person. Without the link, we’d have no way to know whose subscription had changed. Those messages from Google don’t contain payment details either.

Deleting your account doesn’t cancel your subscription. It keeps renewing, and Google keeps charging you, until you cancel it yourself in Google Play: Play Store → your profile → Payments and subscriptions. Travel Kitty can’t cancel it for you, and doesn’t quietly do it on your behalf: the agreement is between you and Google. If you’re leaving, cancel there as well as deleting here, or you’ll keep paying for an app you no longer have.

Deleting your account does remove the records described above.

How long we keep it

Until you delete it. Deleting an expense removes it for everyone on the trip. Deleting a trip you own removes it, its expenses and its photos for everyone on it. Deleting your account is covered below.

Google Analytics records, if you said yes, work differently: they expire after two months, and deleting things in the app doesn’t remove them. See Counting how the app is used.

Otherwise, we keep your information for as long as your account is active. If an account hasn’t been used for a long time, we may remove its data, rather than keep someone’s records forever after they’ve stopped using Travel Kitty. We’ll email the address on the account before removing anything.

A photo you take in Travel Kitty is also saved to your own phone. It goes into your photo library, in an album called Travel Kitty, the moment you take it. So it’s backed up by whatever already backs up your photos, and it stays yours whatever happens to your account or to Travel Kitty. That copy isn’t ours, and we can’t reach it. Photos you pick from your library are already there, so they aren’t copied again. Receipts are different: a receipt you scan stays inside Travel Kitty, and isn’t saved to your photo library.

Photos are the exception, and only on a paid plan. Storing photos and receipts on our servers is part of what a subscription pays for. In what follows, a backed-up receipt counts as a photo.

If a subscription ends, we may remove uploaded photos from our servers after a period, rather than store them forever for an account that’s no longer subscribed. We don’t set that period here, because it depends on how much is being stored and for how many people. Whatever we choose, two things hold. We’ll email the address on the account before removing anything, and you’ll have a reasonable chance to save copies first. For photos taken in Travel Kitty, there’s already a copy in your own library, put there when you took it.

That applies to photos only. Ending a subscription doesn’t by itself remove your trips, expenses, amounts, categories or settlements. Those follow the same rule as everything else above, and stay while the account is in use. Going back to the Free plan is no reason to delete anybody’s record of a trip.

Deleting your account and data

In the app, go to Settings → Profile → Delete account. This permanently removes:

  • your profile;
  • your custom categories;
  • your place search count and your receipt scan count;
  • your receipts;
  • your sign-in details;
  • every trip you own, including its expenses and photos, for everyone on it.

Trips someone else owns are left alone. You’re removed from them, and the expenses you added to them stay, because the trip’s balances wouldn’t add up without them.

It also removes the subscription records described above: the purchase token, the plan, its state and expiry, and the link between the token and your account.

It also clears your Akahu tokens from the phone you delete the account on. If you added them on another phone too, remove them there. Deleting your Travel Kitty account doesn’t end Akahu’s access to your bank: do that in your Akahu account.

Deleting your account doesn’t remove Google Analytics records, if you said yes. They aren’t linked to your account, and they expire after two months.

It doesn’t cancel a Premium subscription, and nothing here can. That’s an agreement between you and Google. It renews, and Google charges you, until you cancel it in Google Play: Play Store → your profile → Payments and subscriptions. Do that as well, in whichever order you like, or you’ll keep paying for an app you’ve deleted. See Subscriptions and payment.

If you can’t get into the app, the account deletion page explains how to ask for deletion by email.

Your rights

You can see everything Travel Kitty keeps about your account inside the app, and on Premium, export a trip’s expenses to a CSV file from Settings. You can also correct anything by editing it, and delete any of it. Google Analytics records aren’t linked to your account, so we can’t look them up for you. You can stop any more being counted at any time in Settings. On either plan, if you’d rather ask for a copy or a correction by email, write to support@travelkitty.nz.
